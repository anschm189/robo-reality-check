Robot-Reality Check

Measuring Progress Against Sci-Fi Dreams
Half-day workshop at ICRA 2027 · Seoul, Korea

14:00–18:00 afternoon 4 capability axes 5 expert talks 20 posters

01About

Each invited speaker holds their own system against a fictional archetype from their domain: the autonomous vehicles of Minority Report, Baymax, the exoskeletons of Elysium, the humanoids of Ex Machina. Together we discuss what current systems already match, where hard limits remain, and what we should actually want going forward, treating fiction not only as a target but as a cautionary tale about surveillance, emotional labor, and equity.

02Capability Axes

The workshop is organized around four capability axes central to robotics, each widely explored in sci-fi media, often far ahead of what current technology delivers. Every 30-minute session pairs a 20-minute expert talk with a 10-minute structured audience discussion. Each discussion opens with a short ethical prompt tied to the axis, so ethical implications arise in every session rather than a single talk.

1 · Learning & Decision-Making
Sci-fi touchstone: Johnny 5 in Short Circuit
How far are learned policies, vision-language-action models, and imitation learning from the fully autonomous, self-directed agents fiction takes for granted?
Ethical promptWho bears the risk when an autonomous system decides?
2 · Hardware & Actuation
Sci-fi touchstone: the exoskeleton in Elysium
Where do actuators, power density, and structural materials actually stand against the effortless, human-amplified bodies of fiction?
Ethical promptWho has access to capability-amplifying technology, and who is left behind?
3 · Manipulation & Physical Assistance
Sci-fi touchstone: Baymax in Big Hero 6
How close are dexterous manipulation and care robots to the gentle, reliable physical assistance fiction has promised for decades?
Ethical promptWhat is lost, or gained, when a robot's read of your needs becomes the basis for a care decision?
4 · Human Perception & Trust
Sci-fi touchstone: Robot & Frank
What can perception, social cognition, and trust calibration in today's robots really do, and where do they hit hard limits in long-term human coexistence?
Ethical promptIs a robot's read of your emotional state a service or a form of surveillance?

03The Shared Capability Table

The workshop's central deliverable

Throughout the afternoon, the audience fills in a shared table built at the front of the room: for each axis, what current systems already match, where hard limits remain, and in the final panel, what we should actually want going forward. aim to summarize the workshop findings in a report or paper and the resulting capability table is designed to be updatable: each future edition can be compared against the previous one, turning the workshop into a small longitudinal benchmark of the sci-fi robotics gap.
